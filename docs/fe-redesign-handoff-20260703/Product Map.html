The product, in one thought

Ventas turns human extracted teeth into released biomaterial. This system is the single thread of traceability and control across that journey.

Every screen today is a slice of one continuous flow — dentin arrives from a partner clinic, moves through a defined production workflow, passes a safety gate, and ships as finished goods. The current UI cut that thread into disconnected, database-shaped pages. This map re-joins it.

HET
Human Extracted Teeth — a sealed container of dentin collected from a partner dental clinic. The raw material.
WORK ORDER
One atomic unit of work in a workflow — a batch moving down a factory line, phase by phase.
WORKFLOW / LINE
One of several product lines — each with its own ordered phase recipe. Every work order runs inside exactly one.
BET
Bacterial Endotoxin Test — the sterility/safety evidence that gates release. Nothing ships unproven.

The connective tissue

Genealogy is the thread that ties all five stages together. Every lot remembers its parents and children — so a finished good traces back to the exact clinic-collected tooth, and any recall or investigation can walk the chain in either direction. The current UI treats genealogy as a buried detail field; it should be a first-class way to navigate the whole system.

Workflow = context

The floor runs several workflows (product lines) in parallel — A-Graft and four others — each defining its own ordered phases, BOM and equipment. A work order lives in exactly one. So workflow isn't just another entity in the model: it's the lens the whole app is scoped by. Pick a line and the board, the operator queue and the QA gates all filter to it; a manager can widen to all lines at once.

People & jobs

Six people, six questions

Each role opens the app to answer one thing. Design the home and the primary screens around these questions — not around the tables the data happens to live in. The two most frequent questions are what the whole product should optimise for.

Production Operator
“What do I work on next?”
Lives in My queue — the next phase tasks assigned to them
QA Reviewer / Releaser
“What can I release today?”
Lives in Release queue — gates ready to sign off
Production Manager
What's blocked, and what's flowing?
Lives in Production board — all WOs by phase
Procurement / Supply
What's coming in from clinics?
Lives in Collections — issuance & returns
Inventory / Warehouse
Where is everything, and where did it come from?
Lives in Inventory + Traceability
Admin / Owner
Is it configured right, and who has access?
Lives in Setup — workflows, users, roles
Diagnosis

Why it doesn't flow today

The UI grew a page at a time as the backend shipped features. The result mirrors the database, not the work. Four symptoms, all fixable.

01

Navigation mirrors tables, not jobs

The menu mixes a workflow (Production), one role's queue (QA), two domains (Procurement, Inventory) and admin — flattened side by side. Nothing signals that they're one flow.

02

Schema leaks onto every screen

Detail pages surface raw keys — unit:HET-…, supply:CLI-…, Legacy serial, Inferred from legacy deliver. Users read foreign keys instead of meaning.

03

No real dashboarding

The home is a wall of counts. It never answers the two questions that actually matter — what do I work on next and what can I release today — for the person looking at it.

04

The thread is invisible

Collection, production, QA and inventory are deeply connected in reality, but the UI never lets you walk from a finished good back to its clinic-collected tooth. Genealogy is buried.

The proposal

A clean information architecture

Re-group everything around the flow and the jobs. Five areas that read like the material journey, a role-aware home that answers the two questions on open, and legacy fields demoted to a collapsible “source record.” A workflow switcher at the top scopes the whole app to one product line.

Principle 01

Follow the material

Screens read in the order the dentin actually moves: Collect → Process → Gate → Release. The IA is the journey.

Principle 02

Answer the question, then show the record

Every screen opens with the job's answer up top. The underlying data — and any legacy fields — sit below or behind a disclosure.

Principle 03

Make the thread walkable

From any lot, WO or finished good you can trace parents and children in one click. Traceability is navigation, not a footnote.

Principle 04

Scope to one line

A workflow switcher sits above everything. Boards, queues and gates default to the active product line, with an “all lines” view for managers.
